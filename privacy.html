Roomsmith Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 September 2026

Roomsmith keeps your rooms on your iPhone. The only things that leave it are the pieces needed to make a picture, and they leave only at the moment you ask for one. There is no account, no analytics and no advertising in this app.

What stays on your phone

Room scans, the photographs taken while scanning, floor plans, your furniture library and every generated picture are stored in the app's own database on the device. They are not copied to a server, and there is no sign-in that could gather them up.

The only setting the app remembers outside that database is the address of the picture service, which is blank unless you deliberately point the app at your own.

What leaves your phone, and when

Nothing is sent while you scan, arrange furniture or look around a room. Making a picture is what sends data, and it sends this:

  • The brief you wrote — the sentence describing how the room should look.
  • A grey render of the scan — a picture of the room's measured shape, which sets the viewpoint and the layout.
  • The product pictures for the furniture placed in that design.
  • A photograph of your room, but only when the design you asked for is built on one of the photographs taken during the scan. A design made from the scan alone sends no photograph.

Two other things reach the service when you use the features that need them. Asking for design ideas sends the facts of the room — what kind of room it is, its measurements, how many windows and doors it has, and a list of the furniture the scan found — to a language model. Adding furniture from a shop link sends that link, and the service then visits that page itself and copies the product pictures from it. The same language model is also used to read a sentence you type to correct a scan, where the app offers that.

What is not sent, in any of this: your name, your email address, your location, your contacts, your photo library as a whole, or any identifier for you or your device.

Who else sees it

The picture service is a thin relay run by the developer. It holds the API key so the app does not have to, and it stores nothing of its own — no database, no copies, no accounts. It runs on Vercel, which handles the connection as any web host would.

From there, everything above goes to fal.ai, which is where the image and language models actually run. Making a picture uses Google's Nano Banana 2 or OpenAI's GPT-Image 2 through fal; the room facts and typed sentences go to a Google Gemini model through fal. These are the only third parties that receive anything. They receive it only to produce what you asked for, and they are required to give it the same protection this policy describes.

Nothing is ever sold, and nothing is shared with anyone else.

How those pictures are held at fal

This is the part of Roomsmith that deserves the plainest language, so here it is plainly. When a picture is made, the images described above are uploaded to fal's content network, and fal hands back a web address for each one. Those addresses are public. fal's own documentation puts it this way: "Media URLs returned by fal (https://v3b.fal.media/...) are publicly accessible — anyone with the URL can access the file until it expires." The addresses are long and random, and they are never published anywhere, so in practice they are known only to the app, to the service and to fal. But there is no password and no permission check on them. A stranger holding the link would be handed the file.

Making them private was tried, and it cannot be done. fal can mark a file so that strangers are refused, and that part works. What breaks is generation: measured on 17 September 2026 against the very model this app uses, fal's image models cannot read a file that has been restricted, and the request fails outright. A restricted file is unreadable even to the account that owns it. So the choice is a picture that can be made or an upload that is private, and it is not currently possible to have both. This page will say otherwise the day that changes.

What can be controlled is how long the files last, and that is set as short as the work allows. Every image the service hands to fal — the room photograph, the scan render, the product pictures, and the finished picture the model returns — is uploaded with a request that it be deleted after one hour. Left alone, fal would keep them for sixty days. An hour is far more than the app needs, since it uploads, composes and downloads in a single run and never refers to those addresses again.

So the honest summary is this: a photograph of your room reaches a third party, is readable by anyone holding the link, and is deleted an hour later.

What the app asks permission for

The camera, because scanning a room uses the camera and the depth sensor together. The local network, only if you point the app at a service running on your own machine. When you add furniture from your photo library, iOS shows you its own picker and hands the app only the pictures you chose — the app never gets the library itself.

No tracking, no analytics, no advertising

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifier and no tracking software of any kind in Roomsmith. The app carries no third-party code at all. Nothing measures how you use it, and nothing about you is profiled or shared for advertising.

Keeping and deleting things

Everything on the phone is yours to remove. Deleting a picture removes it, deleting a room removes that room and its scans, photographs and pictures, and deleting the app removes all of it at once. None of that needs a request to anyone, because there is no copy elsewhere to ask about.

The relay service holds nothing, so there is nothing there to delete. The only copies outside your phone are the short-lived ones at fal described above, and they go on the schedule set out there without anyone having to ask. If you have a question about a particular picture, write to the address below.

There is no consent to withdraw in the usual sense: nothing is collected in the background, so stopping the app from sending anything is a matter of not asking it for a picture, and uninstalling ends it entirely.

Children

Roomsmith is not directed at children and collects nothing that would identify anyone, of any age.

Changes

If what the app does with your data changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves.
